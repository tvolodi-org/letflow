# T-1567 — Design note: fully automated tenant creation (a user registers, the platform creates the tenant and its Keycloak realm with sign-in, no hand work)

Business analyst in the architect's seat. Owner decision relayed by the PM, 2026-11-09 evening (D-115 item 1). Documents only: no code, no route, no setting is changed by this note. The operator copies it to the portfolio `proposals/`.

Question: what exists today, what is the target flow, what are the security rules, how do DNS and certificates fit, what are the slices, and what must the owner decide?

Decision (for UAT and as a proposal for the owner): **build self-service sign-up as a new public step in the front door (the portfolio's landing service), not inside the Letflow backend.** The front door runs a resumable sequence over two things that already exist: infra's realm provisioner and Letflow's `/admin/v1` create route. Letflow gains no Keycloak admin credential and no public tenant-creating route. The first release creates only demo-tier tenants (14 days); production stays a platform-operator step. This follows decision 0072 D1, D3, D6 and the production refusal of P14; it does not change them.

## 2. The target flow, step by step

Actors: the visitor; the **front door** (new public sign-up service, owned by the portfolio); the **provisioner** (infra); **Letflow**; the shared mail relay. State lives in the front door's own table keyed by `tenant_id` (a UUID the front door generates once per sign-up). Every step is idempotent on that key and recorded, so a crash resumes at the first unfinished step.

| # | Step | Detail | Idempotent how | Failure handling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sign-up form | Fields: e-mail, company name, language (ru, kk, en), acceptance of the terms. **No password in the form** (see step 8). A magic link is the proof of e-mail, so no password is stored by the front door. | n/a | field errors in the visitor's language, nothing stored |
| 2 | Anti-abuse gate | Rate limit per client address and per e-mail; a captcha; a disposable-e-mail domain block list; one open sign-up per e-mail; a global cap on pending sign-ups. Checked **before** any mail is sent. | a repeat from the same e-mail while a sign-up is pending resends the same link, no second record | a refusal answers the same neutral text whether or not the e-mail is known (INV-5 style: no account probing) |
| 3 | E-mail verification | The front door mails a single-use link, valid 24 hours, to the address. Nothing is created in Keycloak or Letflow before the link is followed. | the link carries the sign-up id; following it twice is the same as once | expired link: a new request restarts at step 2 |
| 4 | Reserve identity | After the link: the front door generates `tenant_id` (UUID) and derives the **display name** from the company name. The slug is the derived `t-<32 hex>` (S1 D5a). A human-readable subdomain is an owner question (section 7, Q4). | the row keyed by `tenant_id` is written first | a taken hostname: pick again, nothing created yet |
| 5 | Create the realm | Front door calls the provisioner with `tenant_id`, hostname, owner e-mail (verified), language, SMTP settings. Realm created from template v1 in **one** request: hardening, roles without `PLATFORM_ADMIN`, client `letflow-web` with this tenant's own redirect URIs and web origins, brute-force protection on, password policy, e-mail verification on, self-registration off, first administrator inline with a verified e-mail. Returns `realm`, `issuer`, once `initial_password`. | provisioner is idempotent on `tenant_id`; the realm name is deterministic (`t-` plus first 20 hex) | provisioner error or timeout: retry with the same `tenant_id`; after the retry budget the sign-up is marked failed and an alarm is raised, the visitor sees "we will e-mail you" |
| 6 | Create the tenant | Front door calls Letflow **once**: `POST /admin/v1/tenants` with `tenant_id`, name, language, `tier: demo`, `owner_email`, `expires_at` (or the default of 14 days), `hostname`, `idp_realm_id` (as returned). Letflow creates the tenant row and schema, binds the realm, stores the pending `TENANT_ADMIN` grant. | replay by `tenant_id` returns 200 and completes any missing part | 5xx: retry the same call; a 409 `demo_cap_reached` stops the flow (Q1) |
| 7 | Default modules | After step 6 the front door (or a Letflow post-create step; Q5) installs the default modules. **This does not exist today.** Needs a decision where it runs and which actor installs (the provisioner credential must not create roles other than the one grant, S1 P5, so the install cannot run under it). Recommended: a Letflow-internal step inside the same create call, with a fixed list from a setting. | `installed?/2` check before each install | a module that fails to install does not fail the sign-up: the tenant is usable, the failure is logged and reported to the operator |
| 8 | First credential | Two workable paths. (A) the one-time `initial_password` mailed to the person with a required password change at first sign-in; (B) the person chooses a password on a Keycloak page through a "set your password" action link. Path A depends on Keycloak required actions, which S1 section 11 lists as **not tested**. Recommended: test A in the infra sandbox first; fall back to B. The password is never shown on a web page and never stored by the front door. | one-time value: absent on a repeat call; a repeat resends the link only | lost mail: "send again" re-issues through the provisioner, rate limited |
| 9 | Welcome mail | Sent by the front door (never by Keycloak, S1 section 10) after step 6 succeeds: link `https://<hostname>/?realm=<slug>` (see section 5), what to do next, when the demo ends, the contact address. In the person's language. | one mail per sign-up id; resend on request | mail bounce: recorded; the sign-up stays complete |
| 10 | First sign-in | The person signs in through the tenant's own realm. Letflow binds the pending grant (verified e-mail, exact match) and makes them `TENANT_ADMIN`. The wizard of module settings opens. | existing 0048 behaviour | if the grant stays open: runbook S2 "If a grant stays open" |
| 11 | Plan hook | Record the plan chosen (demo only in release 1) and the end date. No payment in release 1. A later paid plan calls the same hook. | one row per tenant | n/a |

Timing (target, to be measured, not promised): steps 5 and 6 are seconds each; the person sees "check your mail" at once and the finished tenant within about a minute of following the link. Step 5 on a real Keycloak was **not** measured over a long run (S1 section 11).

**Rollback and resume.** The sequence is a saga with no distributed transaction. Order is fixed: realm first, tenant second, then the rest (S1 D3). The forward direction is the repair:
* After step 5 fails: nothing exists in Letflow; retry.
* After step 5 succeeded and step 6 failed: an orphan realm exists for a moment; retry step 6 with the same `tenant_id`. If the sign-up is abandoned, the infra reaper removes realms that no tenant claims after a grace period (S1 D3 "a realm without a tenant is only an orphan the reaper removes").
* After step 6 succeeded and a later step fails: the tenant is good. Do not delete anything; repeat the failed step. The tenant row, schema and binding are never undone by hand (S2 "Do not bind or edit the tenant row by hand").
* A sign-up that never completes is closed by the front door after 7 days (UAT assumption, a setting) and, if a tenant exists, handled by the normal expiry sweep.

## 4. DNS and certificates

* Each tenant gets one hostname `<label>.<domain>`. Letflow validates it (format, exactly one label before an allowed suffix from `LETFLOW_PROVISIONER_ALLOWED_HOSTNAME_SUFFIXES`, unique). No suffix is built in; the production demo domain is **not decided** (portfolio O-15).
* **Infra side:** one wildcard DNS record `*.<domain>` pointing at the reverse proxy and one wildcard certificate for `*.<domain>`, renewed by infra. With a wildcard no per-tenant DNS or certificate step exists, so nothing in the sign-up flow waits on infra after the first setup. The provisioner puts the hostname into the realm's redirect URIs and web origins (`https://<hostname>/*`-style exact values from the validated host, never `*`).
* **How this fits `?realm=` and the default tenant slug:** today the browser app picks the tenant by `?realm=<slug>`, a remembered slug, or `LETFLOW_DEFAULT_TENANT_SLUG` (one global value, S3 O5). There is **no host-to-tenant binding** (`?host=` falls back, S6). So a tenant hostname alone does not select the tenant. Until that gap is closed, the welcome link must be `https://<hostname>/?realm=<slug>`. The default tenant slug stays a single global value and cannot serve many sign-ups; it is for the demo instance only.
* Consequence: the redirect URIs derived from the hostname only work if the browser really arrives on that hostname. If every tenant used the shared origin with `?realm=`, redirect URIs would be identical for all realms and a hostname per tenant would be cosmetic. Owner choice in Q4: shared origin plus `?realm=` (works now, no wildcard needed) or per-tenant host (nicer, needs host-to-tenant resolution in Letflow, slice SL6).

## 7. Open owner questions

Q1. **How many open sign-ups may exist?** The cap is 30 live demo tenants. A public form with a cap of 30 is full after one bad day and blocks real prospects. Choices: raise the cap and add a per-source budget (SL5); keep 30 and make sign-up invitation-only (a code); or a waiting list when full. My recommendation: invitation code for release 1, cap unchanged, open sign-up only after the abuse controls (SL3) are live.
Q2. **Plans, pricing and billing.** Which plan names, which limits (users, storage, modules), when does a demo convert, does a paid plan use the same flow? Nothing exists; release 1 records "demo, 14 days" only.
Q3. **Captcha and e-mail reputation.** Which captcha provider (cost, privacy, availability in Kazakhstan), and who maintains the disposable-e-mail list?
Q4. **Tenant address.** (a) shared origin plus `?realm=<t-hex>` (works now, ugly), (b) shared origin plus `?realm=<company-chosen-name>` (needs a slug rule: allowed characters, minimum length, a reserved list such as `www`, `api`, `admin`, `auth`, `platform`, `bpm-default`, `showroom`, which **does not exist today**), or (c) a per-tenant host (SL6). My recommendation: (a) for release 1, (c) as the second release; do not let visitors choose names until a reserved list and a rule exist.
Q5. **Which modules are installed by default?** The product holds assets, locations, codes, catalog items, stock, procurement, organisation and exam modules. The 2026-10-31 direction makes assets and stock counting the application; my proposal is that default set (assets, locations, codes, catalog items), nothing else. Exam is explicitly not a priority (D-043). This is the owner's call.
Q6. **Terms of service and privacy text**, and the retention statement shown at sign-up (14 days demo, 30 days kept, then purged). Who writes it, in which languages? Legal review is outside the team.
Q7. **E-mail sending.** Which relay and sender domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), who owns bounce handling, and whether verification and welcome mail may use the same relay as the realm's account mail. Without it nothing in steps 3, 8, 9 can be tested end to end.
Q8. **Production demo domain** (portfolio O-15) and whether a wildcard certificate is acceptable to the owner's security rules.
Q9. **Erasure on request and self-service deletion.** Who may delete a tenant, is the 30-day retention shown to the person, and what happens to data on a request to erase it earlier.
Q10. **Language of the first release** (ru, kk, en all, or ru and en first) and the support contact printed in the mail.
Q11. **Is production approval for this flow in scope at all?** Everything here is QA only until the owner approves production (D-019 item 3).
