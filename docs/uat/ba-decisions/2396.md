# BA decision for UAT: may the assessor of an escalated review also approve the same loan? (GH #2396)

Business analyst: meridian loan origination (UAT).

## Question

On one meridian loan, can the person who recorded an escalated assessment also be an approver of the same loan?

- The credit director records `credit_decision` in `credit-memo-escalation-review` and may then approve at `l2-approval`. Both nodes route to `role-credit-director`.
- The CRO records `risk_rating` in `risk-assessment-escalation-review` and may then act at the new L1 escalation `escalate-l1-approval-to-cro`. All three route to `role-cro`.

Is that acceptable as it stands, given that a second independent approver always remains? If not, which rule should apply, and does it need a guard change?

## Facts checked on main

I read `test/fixtures/qa/meridian_loan_origination_process_definition.json` (v1.16) myself:

- `credit-memo-escalation-review` has role `role-credit-director`.
- `l2-approval` has role `role-credit-director`. Its `distinct_from` is `["l1-approval", "escalate-l1-approval-to-cro"]`. It does not name the assessment nodes.
- `risk-assessment-escalation-review` has role `role-cro`.
- `escalate-l1-approval-to-cro` has role `role-cro`.
- `l1-approval` has role `role-credit-approver-l1`, with escalation role `role-cro`. It is a different role from both pairs.
- The payment nodes `disburse-loan` and `escalate-disburse-loan-to-loan-ops-lead` list both escalation reviews and `kyc-manual-review` in `distinct_from`, together with the other assessment nodes and all approval nodes. Whoever assessed a loan therefore cannot release its money.
- Both reviews are reached only when the earlier assessment step timed out (the v1.9 / ISS-1015 escalation). The description says "a timeout means more scrutiny".

I did not run anything. This is a reading of the definition.

## Decision (UAT only)

**The pattern is acceptable in UAT as it stands.** The meridian UAT keeps these rules:

1. A credit director who recorded the escalated credit-memo assessment may also approve at `l2-approval` on the same loan.
2. The CRO who recorded the escalated risk assessment may also act at the L1 escalation `escalate-l1-approval-to-cro` on the same loan.
3. UAT scenarios do not expect a refusal for either pair. A scenario may use the same person for both steps, but must not claim that the platform blocks it.
4. No guard or definition change is needed for UAT. No requirement is filed from this decision.

## Reasons

- **A second independent approver always remains.** `l2-approval` names `l1-approval` and `escalate-l1-approval-to-cro` in `distinct_from`, and `l1-approval` is held by a different role (`role-credit-approver-l1`). The CRO's L1 escalation is followed by `l2-approval`, which is named in the same way. Two different people therefore sign off, and one person never decides a loan alone.
- **The assessor is not a payer.** The step with the most risk, releasing money, already refuses anyone who assessed the loan (v1.16, Q-1051 / GH #2395).
- **The assessment is already scrutiny, not a decision.** The escalation exists so that a missed credit memo or risk assessment gets a more senior look. The same senior person using that knowledge at approval is normal credit practice.
- **Forcing a split would cost more than it gains here.** Naming these pairs in `distinct_from` creates same-role pairs, which the shipped-definition guard forbids (issue #2396). Avoiding that would mean routing the approval to another role. That is a role design change, which is out of proportion for a low-rank UAT question.
- **Separation of duties stays on the four sensitive actions.** Approve (the L1 and L2 pair), pay (the payment nodes) and the committee votes are separated by the current definition. The assessor-then-approver pair is a lesser case.

## Open points for the process owner

- UAT assumption, to be confirmed by the process owner: an assessor may approve the same loan. In production the process owner configures this rule. If a real bank requires strict separation, the owner decides the rule (for example a different role for approval). That would be a new requirement with its own change to the shipped-definition guard, and is not decided here.
- UAT assumption, to be confirmed by the process owner: "two different approvers" is enough independence for assessor-then-approver cases. I found no written source that sets a stricter or looser bar.
