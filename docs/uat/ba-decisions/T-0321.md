# BA decision for UAT: which role lets a SwiftRoute dispatcher start a shipment or a driver incident (process audit T-0295, D4)

Business analyst: swiftroute (UAT).

File name: the task carried no GitHub issue number, so this file is named after the task (T-0321). The engine may rename it to the issue number when it closes the question.

## Question

The dispatchers `actor-swiftroute-lena` and `actor-swiftroute-tobias` hold only TASK_WORKER. TASK_WORKER does not hold the permission to start a process instance, so step 1 of five swiftroute scenarios is refused: `driver-incident-assess-and-estimate`, `refusal-driver-incident-confidential`, `refusal-shipment-approval`, `shipment-high-value-happy`, `shipment-ops-timeout-escalation`. In the two refusal scenarios this can give a false PASS (the run stops at step 1 and the later refusals are never really tested).

Which built-in role, or app-side routing role, should a dispatcher hold to start a shipment and an incident (least privilege)? Who may advance the timer in `shipment-ops-timeout-escalation`? What should the roster say?

## Decision (UAT only)

1. **A routing role cannot do this.** Routing roles (`role-ops-manager`, `role-ceo`, `role-accountant`) only decide who may work a task. They carry no permission to start an instance. Only a built-in role can.
2. **The dispatcher who starts a shipment or an incident holds PROCESS_OPERATOR in addition to TASK_WORKER.** Among the built-in roles that hold the start permission, PROCESS_OPERATOR is the least powerful that fits a person running live processes day to day. PROCESS_DESIGNER also starts instances but can write definitions and bind roles, which a dispatcher never needs. TENANT_ADMIN and PLATFORM_ADMIN are far wider and a tenant person must not hold PLATFORM_ADMIN.
3. **Only `actor-swiftroute-lena` receives it.** `actor-swiftroute-tobias` stays TASK_WORKER only. Reason: PROCESS_OPERATOR also reads every instance and the audit log tenant-wide (matrix rows InstancesRead, AuditRead). In `refusal-driver-incident-confidential` Tobias is the dispatcher with "no part in handling" the incident, who must be refused the driver's report. If he were an operator, that refusal could stop being true, or pass only for a reason other than the one the scenario states. He must remain the uninvolved dispatcher.
4. **Consequence for the scenarios:** `shipment-ops-timeout-escalation` should be started by Lena, not Tobias (its actor `dispatcher` points at Tobias today). The other four already use Lena. The scenario edit is a separate UAT-design task; I did not edit scenarios here.
5. **Who advances the timer:** `InstancesAdvanceTimer` is held by PLATFORM_ADMIN, PROCESS_OPERATOR and TENANT_ADMIN, not by TASK_WORKER. The timer step is test control (the 2-hour wait is skipped), not a business act, so it is not a persona's step. It is done by UAT-RUNNER with a swiftroute tooling account that holds PROCESS_OPERATOR. It should not be Lena (a persona would be seen advancing time), not Alice (TENANT_ADMIN would be doing a non-administrative step), and never PLATFORM_ADMIN (tenant-side tests do not use platform authority). The scenario keeps its `actor-system-*`/system step and its existing note that the step is SKIP if the endpoint is unavailable.
6. **What the roster should say** (to be changed by the task that edits the roster, after the owner's approval below):
   - `actor-swiftroute-lena`: `builtin_roles: [TASK_WORKER, PROCESS_OPERATOR]`, note "Dispatcher; starts shipments and incidents; PROCESS_OPERATOR is the least built-in role holding the start permission (ba-decision T-0321)".
   - `actor-swiftroute-tobias`: unchanged, TASK_WORKER only, note that he is the uninvolved dispatcher and must not start instances; his start step moves to Lena.
   - The seed script `scripts/seed_swiftroute_persona_actors.sh` grants PROCESS_OPERATOR to Lena and no more; the roster and the seed must agree.
   - The timer tooling account is not a persona and is not in the roster until infra provides one (see assumptions).

## Reasons

- **Least privilege, from the written matrix.** The grant is the smallest built-in role that holds the permission the step needs, and it goes to one person, not both dispatchers.
- **Owner approach (2026-10-22), as given in the task:** roles are decided and tested through the product's own admin processes, never widened to make a step pass. The roster change is therefore not made by editing the roster alone: the account's real role in the swiftroute realm is assigned through the tenant's own user and role administration, and the roster follows what the realm holds.
- **No false PASS.** Until the role is assigned, step 1 of the five scenarios must be reported BLOCKED (refused at start), not PASS. The two refusal scenarios must not count as passed on the strength of a refusal that came from step 1.
- **Refusals stay genuine.** PROCESS_OPERATOR holds the permission to complete tasks tenant-wide, but the routing-role checks stay in force (`docs/roles.md`), so Lena is still refused at the Operations Manager's and CEO's approvals because she holds neither routing role. The scenarios `refusal-shipment-approval` steps 2 and 5 therefore still test the intended rule; the refusal now rests on the routing role instead of on missing task rights, and its wording should say so.

## Assumptions (UAT assumption, to be confirmed by the process owner)

- UAT assumption: giving Lena PROCESS_OPERATOR in the swiftroute QA realm is acceptable to the owner. This is an access change in the UAT realm, so it needs the owner's written APPROVE to infra (the same precedent as the TENANT_AUDITOR note in the roster). Nothing is assigned by this decision.
- UAT assumption: PROCESS_OPERATOR's tenant-wide InstancesRead lets Lena see all swiftroute instances. This is acceptable for a dispatcher in UAT; the process owner decides whether production dispatchers should see every shipment.
- UAT assumption: an instance-read does not expose the incident's driver name to Tobias through any route today (he is TASK_WORKER and holds InstancesRead per the matrix). I did not check field-level rules for the incident; the audit or the UAT run should show whether step 4 of `refusal-driver-incident-confidential` is a real refusal.
- UAT assumption: a swiftroute tooling account with PROCESS_OPERATOR exists or can be provisioned for UAT-RUNNER. I found no such account in `actors.yaml` or the seed script, and I do not invent one. Until it exists, the timer step stays SKIP/BLOCKED.

## Production

No production permission change is decided here. The built-in roles and their permissions are unchanged. How a production dispatcher is granted the start permission is configured by the process owner.

Decision for UAT only; the process owner configures production.
