# BA decision for UAT: SwiftRoute dispatchers Lena and Tobias on QA hold PROCESS_OPERATOR, against the roster and decision T-0321 (GH #3220)

Business analyst: swiftroute (UAT).

## Question

The UAT run T-0521 found that on QA both `actor-swiftroute-lena` and `actor-swiftroute-tobias` hold TASK_WORKER and PROCESS_OPERATOR. The roster (`test/fixtures/uat/actors.yaml`) and the header of `scripts/seed_swiftroute_persona_actors.sh` say TASK_WORKER only. The infra script `qa-uat-env.sh` (lines 23-24, per the issue) gives both PROCESS_OPERATOR. The earlier BA decision `docs/uat/ba-decisions/T-0321.md` (points 3 and 6) gives it to Lena only. Which is intended: (a) apply T-0321, or (b) keep both as operators and rewrite `refusal-driver-incident-confidential`?

## Decision (UAT only)

**Option (a): T-0321 stands.**

1. Lena holds TASK_WORKER and PROCESS_OPERATOR. She is the dispatcher who starts shipments and driver incidents.
2. Tobias holds TASK_WORKER only. He is the dispatcher with no part in handling the incident, and the one who must be refused the driver's incident report.
3. QA must follow this. The change on QA is: in the swiftroute realm, remove PROCESS_OPERATOR from `actor-swiftroute-tobias` and keep TASK_WORKER and PROCESS_OPERATOR on `actor-swiftroute-lena`; `qa-uat-env.sh` line 24 is updated to match. This is an access change in the UAT realm made by the infra owner, so it needs the owner's written approval. Nothing is assigned by this decision.
4. Follow-up work for the team after QA matches (separate tasks, not done here):
   - `actors.yaml` and `scripts/seed_swiftroute_persona_actors.sh`: Lena gets PROCESS_OPERATOR, Tobias is unchanged. The roster and the seed must agree with each other and with QA.
   - `shipment-ops-timeout-escalation`: nothing to do. T-0322 already moved its start step to Lena (the scenario names `dispatcher: actor-swiftroute-lena`).
   - `refusal-driver-incident-confidential` stays as written; it needs no rewrite. Its step 4 already has Tobias (`other_dispatcher`) with `expect_refusal: true`.
5. While QA's roles differ from the roster, step 4 of `refusal-driver-incident-confidential` is not proven and must be reported BLOCKED or not-proven, not PASS. Restoring Tobias to TASK_WORKER only does not by itself make step 4 provable: the permission matrix in `docs/roles.md` gives InstancesRead tenant-wide to TASK_WORKER as well, so whether the step can be a real refusal at all is the product question in #2932, not decided here.

## Reasons

- Option (b) would keep a second dispatcher as an operator on QA, and would mean rewriting a scenario or its persona to fit a drifted state. The org structure puts Tobias beside Lena (`test/fixtures/simulation/swiftroute/org_structure.yaml`, dept-dispatch, members Lena and Tobias), and the scenario already uses him as the uninvolved dispatcher. T-0321 and the owner's approach of 2026-10-22 (roles are decided on least privilege and never widened to make a step pass) point to (a). PROCESS_OPERATOR is a second source of tenant-wide instance read for Tobias; removing it narrows his access, though not to nothing (see point 5).
- Least privilege: only one dispatcher needs the permission to start an instance (T-0321 point 2). Granting it to both is wider than any step requires.
- The roster, the seed script and T-0321 already agree that Tobias is TASK_WORKER only; QA and the infra script are the sources that drifted, and the drift is the defect.
- The product defect that makes the incident readable through InstancesRead is tracked separately in #2932 and is not decided here.
- Meridian shows the same pattern (#3115). That is a separate question for the Meridian BA; this decision does not settle it. The reasoning that carries over is the one used here: least privilege (only the persona who starts instances gets PROCESS_OPERATOR) and agreement of roster, seed and earlier BA decision while QA drifted. Whether a refusal can be real despite the tenant-wide InstancesRead that every TASK_WORKER holds is the product question in #2932, not settled here.
