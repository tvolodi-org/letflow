## Short answer
1. **Put production on its own new small server**, not on a shared host. Estimated EUR 16–40 per month for server and domain parts **[E]**.
2. **First owner decision: is an off-site backup copy allowed?** Today the rule is "local disk only" (fact pack section 2). With that rule, losing the server loses the data and the backups together. This is the biggest risk in the plan and only the owner can accept or lift it.
3. **Before the first paying company:** a monitor that phones someone, a restore drill that has actually been run, and a production Keycloak on Postgres.

## 2. Backups and restore
- **Today [V]:** demo and Qoimeks prod are dumped daily (7 sets) on the same disk; QA has no schedule by decision; no WAL archiving; restore tested once (Qoimeks, 2026-10-08).
- **What a backup must hold** (REQ-785 criterion 4): the PostgreSQL database with every tenant schema, the Keycloak realm of every tenant, and `LETFLOW_SECRETS_MASTER_KEY` kept **apart** from the database copy (decision 0016 A and B: without it stored secrets cannot be read). "A purge does not erase backups or replicas" (`docs/runbooks/tenant-provisioner-credential.md`).
- **Proposal (the infra pack's, taken as a proposal, not measured):** daily dump + local WAL archiving → RPO 15 minutes against a logical error or a bad deploy; RTO 1–2 hours on the same server; a restore drill every month into a throwaway container.
- **Off-site copy (needs the owner's yes):** a second place that is not the same server. Estimated EUR 3–10 per month for tens of GB at current data sizes (the whole demo set is 1.5 MB, Qoimeks 110 KB) **[E: general object-storage price range]**. Without it: host or disk loss = not recoverable. Written acceptance of that risk by the owner is the alternative.
- What D-115(2) delivers is not in this repo yet; this section must be reconciled with it when it lands. No RPO/RTO number is approved: they are open owner questions (REQ-785 criterion 8).

## 3. Sign-in per company
- One company = one tenant = one Keycloak realm (D-016); the realm comes from `priv/keycloak/provisioner/v2/tenant-realm.json`. Until a production provisioner is approved, realms are made by hand per `docs/runbooks/onboarding-new-tenant-realm.md`.
- Realm per tenant vs one realm: keep a realm per tenant. It keeps users, brute-force limits and admin rights apart per company; the price is more realms to back up and to update. One shared realm is cheaper to run but one mistake exposes all companies (UAT assumption, to be confirmed by the owner).
- Keycloak runs on one public auth host, realms are path-based, so **Keycloak needs no wildcard** (demo-operator-inputs.md). The company sites need names like `<company>.letflow.ai-dala.com`: Cloudflare's free certificate does **not** cover two labels under the zone [V section 5]. Recommended: a DNS-only wildcard record plus one free Let's Encrypt wildcard certificate (the same pattern already works for `*.letflow.qa.bizdala.com`). Cost EUR 0 **[V pattern; E price]**. It needs a DNS-edit credential on the server (infra T-0251): a new token restricted to the server's IP, never the existing write token.
- The domain `ai-dala.com` is the owner's; whether production uses it or a new domain is owner decision 3.
- Open: Keycloak manage-users rights for a production tenant's first administrator (D-019 item 7 settles only demo tenants).

## 4. Monitoring and on-call
Today [V]: Docker health checks and systemd timers only; no uptime monitor, no alert channel, nothing reads `/metrics`.
Minimum before production: (1) an outside uptime check of the login page and the API health on every minute-or-five — free tiers exist, EUR 0–10 **[E: general]**; (2) alerts to one channel a person reads (phone message or e-mail), including "backup failed" and "certificate expires in 14 days"; (3) the metrics page scraped and kept for 30 days on the same server (small); (4) logs kept on the server 14 days, no log service. Who is on call (named person and the hours) is an owner decision; until then there is none.

## 5. Move of the Qoimeks pilot
What is known [V section 7]: the Qoimeks pilot runs on hetzner-prod as a separate Go application (`qoimeks-prod` + `qoimeks-db`), database 11 MB, 2 tenant schemas (Client 1: about 600 movements, 600 balances, 666 SKUs, 134 locations, 3 imports; and the owner's test tenant); access is by static access codes, no user accounts; daily dump, no WAL.
**UNKNOWN, ask the owner:** is the target of the move the Letflow assets and stock module (my assumption, UAT assumption, to be confirmed)? Does Client 1 pay? How many end users? Is a pause allowed?
Steps (proposal): (1) freeze a pilot copy and a dump; (2) map the data to Letflow's xlsx import of locations, items and opening balances (an existing import path for assets exists, REQ-784; whether the stock module accepts the same is **not verified**); (3) rehearse on QA with the copy, compare counts (as `demo_counts.sh` does for the demo); (4) create the company's tenant and realm, users by e-mail instead of access codes; (5) cut over in a stated quiet hour, Qoimeks put to read-only; (6) keep the old Qoimeks running read-only 30 days. **Rollback:** switch the name back to Qoimeks and re-enable writes; any entries made in Letflow after cut-over are exported and re-entered by hand. **Who tells the pilot users:** the owner or the portfolio manager, in the users' language (ru/kk), at least a week before and again on the day; Letflow's team writes the text, it does not contact them.
Cost: no extra hosting; mostly effort. Do not move before the monitor, the restore drill and the sign-in per company work.

## 6. Legal and compliance basics
Data location: the QA host's name says nbg1 (Hetzner, Germany; my reading of the host name, not verified), so data on a Hetzner VPS would sit in the EU unless another site is chosen, not in Kazakhstan. Whether the customers' data may leave Kazakhstan is a question for the owner and a lawyer; I did not research Kazakh data-protection law and make no claim about it. Needed before the first paying company: terms of use, a privacy notice naming where data lives and who the sub-processors are (Hetzner, Cloudflare, any off-site storage), a retention rule for backups of a deleted company (backups keep it until they expire), and a contact for data requests. Cost: lawyer time, EUR UNKNOWN.
